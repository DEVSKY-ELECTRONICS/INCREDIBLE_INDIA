01

Rajasthan · The Land of Kings · Multiple UNESCO Sites

Rajasthan Forts

Rajasthan — "Land of Kings" — is a state of extraordinary fortresses, palaces, and desert cities. The Amber Fort in Jaipur glows honey-gold on its hillside, with elephant rides to the gate and mirror-work interiors. Mehrangarh in Jodhpur towers 125m above the "Blue City" — its blue-washed houses spread below like a painting. Jaisalmer's "Golden Fort" is the only inhabited fort in the world, with 5,000 people living and trading inside its sandstone walls. Udaipur's Lake Palace seems to float on Pichola Lake, and is one of Asia's most romantic settings. The Thar Desert offers camel safaris under billion-star skies.

02

Goa · Smallest State · Former Portuguese Colony · Beach Capital

Goa

India's smallest state is its most cosmopolitan. For 450 years under Portuguese rule, Goa developed a unique fusion culture — you find churches and temples side by side, coconut-based seafood curries alongside chorizo, and a relaxed pace that feels unlike anywhere else in India. The 100km coastline ranges from party beaches (Baga, Calangute) to secluded coves (Palolem, Agonda). Old Goa's 16th-century churches — including the UNESCO Basilica of Bom Jesus where St. Francis Xavier's body is preserved — are masterpieces of Portuguese baroque architecture. The December–January season is peak Goa: Christmas and New Year under palm trees with music on the beach.

🏖️ 100km Coastline⛪ UNESCO Churches🐟 Seafood Capital🎵 Music Festivals🛵 Scooter Rides🎄 Christmas on Beach

03

Mumbai · Maharashtra · City of Dreams · Financial Capital

Mumbai

Mumbai — formerly Bombay — is India's most cosmopolitan city, producing 40% of India's tax revenue and the entire global Bollywood film industry. The Gateway of India is a magnificent stone arch built in 1924 to receive King George V, now the embarkation point for ferry rides to Elephanta Island caves. Dharavi — Asia's most densely populated neighbourhood — is also Asia's most industrious, with a billion-dollar recycling and manufacturing economy in just 2 square km. Marine Drive's "Queen's Necklace" lights are iconic. The Dabbawalas deliver 200,000 home-cooked lunches to offices daily with near-zero errors — a Harvard Business School case study.

Goa Sunset at Palolem
